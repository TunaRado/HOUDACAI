Who We Are

About Houdacai

Houdacai is a Florida-based LLC that builds software, hardware, and tools for people who want things that actually work the way they're supposed to.

We're not a big company. We're problem solvers, builders — we find problems that bother us, figure out the right way to solve them, design high utility solutions, and ship. Every product we make gets the same level of care whether it's an app on your phone or something you hold in your hands.

If something we built is useful to you, that's the whole point.

Founders

Houdacai was founded by a Florida-based electronics engineer with roughly 28 years of experience across instrumentation design, embedded systems, UAV ground control, and high-fidelity aviation simulation. The kind of background where you've shipped real hardware, managed safety-critical software programs, and learned what it actually costs when something doesn't work correctly.

The design process here is formal systems engineering. That means before a single requirement is written, the problem space is defined — who actually has the problem, what they're trying to accomplish, what constraints they're operating under, and what a good outcome actually looks like. Use cases come next, grounding the design in real scenarios before any architecture decisions are made. Requirements follow from that, architecture is validated against requirements, and nothing gets built until it traces back to a real need. Every requirement also traces forward to a test — so there's no ambiguity about whether a feature works, it either passes its verification criteria or it doesn't ship. Most small software shops skip straight to building. The result is products that solve the wrong problem correctly, or the right problem poorly. That's not how we work.

The products here aren't side experiments — they come from real gaps. Mindrive exists because half the binaural beat apps out there aren't even in stereo — which means they're not producing binaural beats, they're just making noise.

The approach hasn't changed across any of it: understand the problem correctly before touching the solution, build things that hold up in the field, and don't ship until it's right.
